Ideas

Links that never die.

Anywhere a link or QR code has to outlast the thing it points to. Each one can be pointed somewhere new at any time, or at a simple page you write, with no website needed.

Personal and family

  • Luggage and bag tagsPoint a tag at a page with how to reach you. If your number changes or you lend the bag to someone, update the page instead of the tag.
  • Family and emergency infoA password-protected page with contacts and instructions for babysitters, house-sitters or relatives.
  • Pet, bike and gear tagsA simple “if you found this” note with a way to contact you, which you can change any time.
  • KeepsakesWedding signs, cards and photo books that link to an album you can keep adding to.
  • MemorialsA plaque or headstone that links to a page about the person, which family can keep up to date for years.
  • Home manualsStickers on the furnace, water heater or appliances that link to the manual or service notes. Repoint them when things are replaced.
  • Kids’ art and school projectsLink a drawing or science-fair poster to a video of the story behind it.

Business owners

  • Menus and price listsOne code on the table, pointing at whichever menu is current.
  • Storefront signsHours, booking and contact info that change without repainting the window.
  • Packaging, labels and manualsCodes printed on products that always lead to the latest manual, recall notice or support page.
  • Business cards and brochuresPoint at your current website, portfolio or booking page, even after you switch providers.
  • Real estate signsReuse the same sign rider: point it at whichever listing it’s standing in front of.
  • Events and flyersThe event page beforehand, the photos or recording afterwards.
  • Reviews and feedbackPoint “leave us a review” at whichever review site you use this year.

Organizations and public spaces

  • Trails, parks and historic plaquesSigns that are expensive to replace and meant to last decades.
  • Museum and exhibit labelsLabels that link to audio guides or extra material you can improve over time.
  • Equipment and facilitiesAsset tags linking to manuals, safety sheets or maintenance logs.
  • Members-only infoA code on the clubhouse wall or staff room that opens a password-protected page or link.
  • Schools, churches and clubsPosters and handouts that point at this term’s schedule or newsletter.
  • Reports and citationsLinks in published work that keep working when websites reorganize. No more link rot.

Makers and creators

  • Books and printed mediaCompanion videos, errata or updated resources, linked from pages you can’t reprint.
  • Art and handmade goodsA tag that tells the story of the piece and links to your current shop.
  • Merch and packagingPoint at whatever you’re promoting now: the new album, the next drop.
  • Talks and slidesShow a code on your first slide: the slides today, the recording once it’s up.

Links, not just QR codes

  • Email signatures and biosOne link that never changes, pointing wherever you want people to go right now.
  • Documents already sentPDFs, résumés and proposals can’t be edited once they’re out. Their links can still be updated.
  • Pre-printed batchesMake dozens of codes before you know where they’ll go, print them, and set each one later.
  • AutomationDevelopers can update links from scripts, for example pointing a product’s code at each new release.

Keep in mind

Lock anything private with a password; otherwise anyone can see where a link points. PermaPath never tracks who scans; it can count scans per day, and those counts are public.
